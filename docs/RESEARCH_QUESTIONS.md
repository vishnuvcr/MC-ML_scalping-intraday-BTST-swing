# Research Questions and Scope

## Primary question
Can Monte Carlo methods be significantly and reproducibly useful in generating, validating, sizing or risk-controlling profitable stock-trading systems across scalping, intraday, BTST and swing horizons?

## Operational definition of useful
A Monte Carlo component is useful when, relative to a matched baseline, it produces a pre-specified improvement in out-of-sample economic performance and/or risk control that survives realistic execution costs and appropriate multiple-testing/overfitting controls.

## Sub-questions
- Does trade-sequence randomization provide reliable drawdown and risk-of-ruin estimates?
- Does dependent bootstrap improve inference compared with IID resampling?
- Do permutation/placebo tests distinguish signal from chance more reliably than a naive backtest?
- Does simulated path analysis improve decision quality without creating look-ahead bias?
- Does parameter perturbation reveal robust regions rather than a single optimum?
- Are effects different for scalping, intraday, BTST and swing horizons?
- How much do spread, slippage and brokerage erode any edge?
- Are results specific to a few symbols or broad across a defined stock universe?
